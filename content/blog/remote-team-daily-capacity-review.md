# A daily capacity review for remote operations teams

*August 20, 2026*

## Operating question

Offshore Outsourcing Company frames this work around a Philippines-based operations role with a defined lane, named owner, limited permissions, and a review rhythm. The purpose is not to make a remote specialist responsible for every judgment that appears in a queue. It is to make routine work teachable and make uncertainty visible early. A useful operating record states what entered, which approved source was used, what action was allowed, and who owns the next decision. ## Build the record

Keep the source of truth close to the work. A copied spreadsheet or chat summary may be useful for coordination, but it should point back to the authoritative record and show when it was checked. ## Handle waiting states

Use statuses that describe what happens next. Queued, active, waiting for source, waiting for owner, ready for review, returned, and closed are more useful than a single label such as in progress. Every waiting state needs an owner, a reason, and a follow-up point. Do not use completion to make a dashboard look healthier. A truthful pause protects downstream work from an unsupported assumption. ## Set access boundaries

Access should follow the approved lane, not the seniority of a role. For each system, record whether the specialist may view, create, edit, export, send, approve, or delete. ## Design the handoff

Time-zone work benefits from explicit handoff fields. Capture the last meaningful event, current state, unresolved question, next action, accountable owner, and next review point. Distinguish an internal target from a customer or supplier commitment. If an owner is unavailable, state the impact and the exact decision required instead of forwarding a whole thread. This turns the gap between schedules into a manageable queue state. A backup owner can resume the work without guessing, and the original specialist can see whether the escalation was understood. ## Pilot the routine

Pilot any new routine on a bounded queue. Use a small set of low-risk examples, observe the first outputs, review exceptions, and change one dimension at a time. Do not increase volume, data access, and decision authority simultaneously because a later failure will be hard to explain. Keep the approved scope and examples current. If repeated questions reveal a missing rule, route the proposed change to the process owner.
