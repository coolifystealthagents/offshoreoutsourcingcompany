# Design an Offshore Outsourcing SLA Scorecard

*September 25, 2026*

A service-level scorecard should help a client and provider make decisions. A wall of green percentages can hide rework, aged exceptions, and heavy client intervention. Start with the customer or operational outcome, define the measurement source, and pair speed with quality.

## Start with a decision record

Before collecting documents or scheduling meetings, name the business owner, the decision being considered, the affected workflow, and the evidence needed. Record assumptions separately from verified facts. Use a simple status such as open, confirmed, rejected, or needs specialist review. This prevents a plausible statement from becoming an operating requirement merely because it appeared in a proposal or chat.

The record should also identify the people affected by the change. An outsourcing design touches process owners, system administrators, security and privacy reviewers, finance, managers, and the people doing the work. Consultation does not blur accountability: one authorized owner should still approve scope, access, and customer-impacting decisions.

## Define each measure precisely

For every metric, document the numerator, denominator, clock start, clock stop, business calendar, exclusions, source system, owner, and review frequency. Distinguish response from resolution and completed from accepted. Test the definition against several records before using it commercially.

## Balance speed and quality

Pair throughput or turnaround with first-pass acceptance, factual accuracy, required-field completeness, and correct escalation. Include the client’s approval delay separately from provider processing time. This prevents a team from being penalized for a waiting state it does not control and prevents fast but incomplete work from appearing successful.

## Show distribution and exceptions

An average can hide old work. Report volume bands, percentile turnaround where appropriate, aged items, reopen rates, and exception reasons. Small queues may need record-level review rather than unstable percentages. Annotate material changes in volume, systems, or scope so trend lines retain context.

## Create an action rhythm

Use a short operational review for immediate blockers and a periodic governance review for patterns, capacity, scope, and changes. Every red or disputed measure should produce an owner, next action, and follow-up date. Limit scorecard fields to measures that influence a decision; move diagnostic detail into a linked record.

## Control changes

Metric definitions should have versions and effective dates. When a workflow changes, decide whether the old and new periods are comparable. Do not rewrite prior results using a new rule without preserving the original view. Commercial remedies, if any, should follow the contract and authorized review rather than an analyst’s interpretation.

## A practical review table

Use six columns: question, current evidence, assumption, decision owner, next action, and due date. Add a risk or privacy classification only when it changes handling. Keep links to authoritative systems instead of copying sensitive data into the worksheet. At each review, close answered questions, preserve the decision history, and identify any change that affects cost, access, schedule, service, or customer commitments.

The table is complete enough when another authorized reviewer can understand why the proposed model was accepted or rejected without reconstructing the entire project. It should reveal retained client work as clearly as provider work. It should also show the normal path, the exception path, and the point at which the offshore team must wait for a client decision.

## Sources to use during the decision

Use primary sources as guardrails, then adapt the operating design to the actual systems, contracts, and risk profile. The [NIST Cybersecurity Framework](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) is a useful reference for governance and access questions. The [US Small Business Administration guidance on hiring and managing employees](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/hire-manage-employees) helps frame employer responsibilities. For Philippine personal-data handling, consult the [National Privacy Commission](https://privacy.gov.ph/data-privacy-act/) and qualified counsel. These references do not replace legal, tax, employment, or security advice.

## Common failure modes

Watch for a role title standing in for a workflow, a price standing in for total cost, a certification standing in for configured controls, or a meeting standing in for a decision. Other warning signs include undocumented exclusions, shared accounts, unowned exceptions, metrics without source definitions, and plans that expand volume, permissions, and authority at the same time.

A disciplined process does not eliminate uncertainty. It makes uncertainty reviewable. When evidence is missing, assign the search or specialist review. When the answer changes the commercial or operating model, update the proposal, responsibility map, and approval record together.
