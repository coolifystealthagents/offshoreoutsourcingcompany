# Legal Matter Intake Preparation for a Philippines Support Team

*October 5, 2026*

A new legal inquiry can arrive as a web form, forwarded email, voicemail, referral, or message to an employee. The first operational challenge is not deciding the law. It is preserving the inquiry, identifying the people and organizations involved, and getting the record to an authorized lawyer without creating a false impression that representation already exists.

A Philippines support specialist can administer that path when the firm or legal department defines exactly what may be collected, what must be said, and where judgment begins. Attorneys retain decisions about conflicts, duties, deadlines, privilege, engagement, legal advice, and strategy.

## Treat first contact as an unaccepted inquiry

The intake script should use neutral language. It can explain that information is being collected for review and that submission does not itself create an attorney-client relationship. It should not say that the matter is accepted, predict an outcome, or advise the person what to do.

Record the receipt channel and time, prospective client or internal requester, preferred safe contact route, broad matter type, adverse or related parties named by the requester, relevant organizations, general jurisdiction, known event dates, referral source, and immediate communication needs. Collect only enough narrative to route and screen. A free-form invitation to “tell us everything confidentially” can gather sensitive details before the organization knows whether it can receive them.

If a requester mentions an imminent hearing, filing, expiration, eviction, detention, safety event, or other time-sensitive consequence, the coordinator flags the exact statement and alerts the designated lawyer. The coordinator does not calculate a deadline or reassure the requester that time remains.

## Build names for conflict review without deciding the conflict

Conflict preparation depends on accurate identities. Separate people from organizations and record aliases, former names, parent entities, subsidiaries, counterparties, witnesses, and existing clients only when supplied or found through an approved source. Preserve the requester’s relationship to each name.

Normalization matters. “ABC,” “ABC Holdings LLC,” and a trading name may or may not refer to the same entity. The specialist can search approved systems and present possible matches. The conflicts lawyer decides whether identities are the same, whether a relationship creates a conflict, and whether a waiver or other response is possible.

Keep search results with the screening record. A result should show the term used, system searched, time, candidate matches, and reviewer outcome. A blank result from one spelling is not proof that no conflict exists. The process should require alternate names and a second look when identifiers are incomplete.

## Quarantine documents until routing is clear

People often attach contracts, medical records, financial statements, identity documents, or opposing-party correspondence to first contact. Define an approved restricted location for those files and limit who can open them. The intake coordinator should not redistribute attachments through chat or personal folders.

Scan files through approved security controls and preserve the original name, source, receipt time, and relationship to the inquiry. If an attachment appears corrupted, password-protected, or suspicious, use the security route instead of improvising a workaround. If the organization’s policy prohibits certain materials before screening, the coordinator follows that policy and alerts the intake owner.

Document receipt does not mean the content has been legally reviewed. The record should distinguish received, security-cleared, indexed, attorney review pending, and accepted into an opened matter.

## Separate screening, consultation, and engagement

Use visible gates. The first gate confirms that minimum intake and names are ready. The second records the lawyer’s conflict and suitability decision. A later gate may authorize consultation scheduling. Engagement requires the organization’s approved agreement and acceptance process.

Scheduling a call is not the same as accepting representation. Give coordinators approved language for invitations, declines, requests for more information, and referrals. They must not edit substantive legal wording or explain why a matter was declined unless the responsible lawyer supplies the message.

For an internal legal department, the equivalent gate may decide whether the request belongs with legal, compliance, human resources, security, or another function. The specialist can package the facts and route them; the authorized owner determines classification and legal response.

## Preserve the requester’s words and the staff summary

Store the original message or recording reference separately from the operational summary. The summary can identify parties, event, requested help, locations, and stated dates. It should not introduce a conclusion such as “clear breach,” “fraud,” or “valid claim.”

If a requester corrects a name or date, append the correction rather than silently rewriting history. If two messages conflict, show both and ask the lawyer or requester for clarification under the approved process. This lets the reviewer see what was known at each stage.

Translation and accessibility need their own controls. Record the language used, whether interpretation is requested, and the approved interpreter route. Machine translation may assist routing only if policy permits; it should not quietly become the basis for legal advice or a deadline decision.

## Control access and retention

Matter intake can contain highly sensitive personal and commercial information. Use named accounts, least privilege, access logging, and role-specific queues. Avoid sending full narratives in notification subjects. Remove coordinator access when duties change and apply the organization’s approved retention schedule to declined or incomplete inquiries.

The [American Bar Association Model Rules of Professional Conduct](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/) provide professional-responsibility context for U.S. lawyers, including duties involving prospective clients, confidentiality, supervision, and conflicts. Actual obligations vary by jurisdiction and organization. The [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework) offers voluntary privacy-risk guidance. Qualified counsel must approve the intake design; these sources are not a substitute for that review.

## Review intake as a chain of custody

Sample inquiries from receipt to final disposition. Check whether the original contact is preserved, parties are searchable, sensitive files stayed controlled, urgency statements reached a lawyer, conflict results have a reviewer, communications used approved language, and the final status is accurate.

Useful measures include inquiries acknowledged, minimum records complete, possible-name matches awaiting review, urgent flags reaching the owner, attachments outside the approved store, scheduling before authorization, and records without a final disposition. Faster intake is not better if it creates premature promises or incomplete conflict data.

Pilot with one inquiry type and one supervising lawyer. Use synthetic scenarios involving alternate company names, a deadline claim, an attachment, a former-client match, and a matter the organization will not accept.
